What this guide covers

The right treatment depends on the right identification. Many pests leave signs before you ever see them, and a clear photo or a description of the droppings is often enough to tell which one you are dealing with.

Droppings

What droppings tell you

Size, shape and quantity are the quickest clues. Droppings are rarely a reason to panic, but they do tell us where to look.

By room

Where to look in each room

A systematic look finds more than a quick glance. Use a torch, and look behind and under things.

  • Kitchen: under the sink, behind the fridge and cooker, inside cupboard corners and in the dry food store
  • Bathrooms: around drain covers, behind toilets and under vanity units
  • Bedrooms: mattress seams, headboard, bed frame, behind picture frames
  • Living areas: skirting boards, door frames, behind furniture and curtains
  • Roof space and ceilings: along beams and pipes, near insulation and cable routes
  • Outside: air-conditioner platforms, bins, drains, boundary walls and the garden

Time of day

When you see it can help

At night in the kitchen

Cockroaches. Seeing one by day usually means a large population.

Scratching in ceilings at night

Rodents, often rats if the noise is heavy, mice if it is light.

Insects near lights after dusk

Winged ants or termites, flying insects, or mosquitoes outdoors.

Bites found in the morning

Bed bugs are the usual suspect, though other insects can bite too.

Photos

How to take a photo that helps

A good photo can speed up identification. Try to include:

  • A close-up of the insect, dropping or damage, with a coin or ruler beside it for scale
  • A wider shot showing where it was found
  • More than one angle, with a light that avoids glare
  • A sample of droppings or an insect in a sealed container, if you can
  • The date, the time of day and the room
  • A short note about what you have noticed, such as noises, smells or bites

Avoid

What not to do before help arrives

Some well-meant actions make things worse:

  • Do not spray repeatedly or mix products, as it can scatter insects and is unsafe
  • Do not move bedding or furniture to another room if you suspect bed bugs
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings: dampen them and wear gloves
  • Do not block holes in the hope the pest goes away, as it may be trapped inside
  • Do not disturb termite mud tubes more than necessary
  • Do not throw away evidence such as droppings or damaged wood until we have seen it

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you identify a pest from a photo?

Often, yes. A clear close-up with something for scale, plus a wider shot of where it was found, lets us identify many pests and tell you what to expect. Some need an inspection to confirm.

What should I do with a pest I have caught?

Place it in a sealed container or stick it to clear tape so it stays intact, and show it to us. Do not crush it, because that makes identification harder.

I see droppings but no pest. Is it still worth calling?

Yes. Droppings often mean activity at night. The size and shape tell us what it is, and we can find where it is entering.

Are rodent droppings dangerous?

They can carry contamination. Wear gloves, avoid raising dust, dampen the area before cleaning and wash your hands afterwards. For large amounts, ask us for advice.

Should I spray first and call later?

Spraying can scatter insects and make the problem harder to treat, and it can disturb evidence we need to identify the pest. If you can, call first.
