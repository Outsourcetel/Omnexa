Bed bugs

A fast, discreet response to bed bugs

Isolate

The room is taken out of use and the guest is moved to a room that has been checked.

Inspect

We inspect the room, the neighbouring rooms and the rooms above and below, because bed bugs travel.

Treat and follow up

Beds, frames and furniture are treated, and we return to check after the egg cycle.

Record

The service report records what was found and done, so repeat cases show up.

Housekeeping

What housekeeping teams can look for

Housekeepers see every room. A short routine at turnover catches problems early:

  • Lift the mattress corner and check seams and piping for dark spots or blood marks
  • Look behind the headboard and at the bed frame joints
  • Check luggage racks, upholstered chairs and curtains in rooms with a complaint
  • Report live insects or shed skins at once, without moving bedding to other rooms
  • Seal soiled linen in bags and keep it off the floor
  • Record the room number and date for every report

Discretion

Handling guest complaints quietly

How a hotel handles a pest complaint matters as much as the pest itself. Move quickly, move the guest, and do not discuss the problem in public areas.

We work with your duty manager and housekeeping so that technicians are discreet, use service entrances and avoid leaving visible signs of treatment in guest areas. The details of the room and the finding stay in the service report for your records.

Records

What the hotel's pest control file should hold

A complete file shows inspectors and brand auditors that the property is under control:

  • The contract and scope
  • A site plan with numbered monitoring points
  • A service report for each visit
  • A log of guest and staff reports, and the response to each
  • Details of any bed bug cases, treatment and follow-up
  • Products used and the technician's details

Check your provider

A pest control provider should be ready to show its credentials. Ask any provider to show you its Balady qualification, and check that public health pesticides carry an SFDA registration number on the label.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What should staff do when a guest reports bed bugs?

Apologise, move the guest to another room that has been checked, and do not use the affected room until it is inspected and treated. Call us so we can inspect adjacent rooms as well.

Can treatment happen while the hotel is full?

Yes, in most cases. We plan room-by-room work around occupancy, and rooms stay out of use only for the time we advise.

Do you work with apartment buildings and staff accommodation too?

Yes. Serviced apartments and staff housing have longer stays and more cooking, which changes the programme, and we plan for that.

How do bed bugs get into a hotel?

Mostly in guests' luggage and belongings. They hide in bed frames, headboards and furniture, so even a very clean property can be affected.

Will guests notice the treatment?

Our aim is for them not to. Visits are scheduled to avoid disturbing guests, and our technicians work discreetly.
