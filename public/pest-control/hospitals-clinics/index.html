What we cover

Pest control for healthcare

Healthcare sites have to control pests without putting patients, staff and sensitive areas at risk. That means choosing low-risk methods, agreeing access and timing with your infection control and facilities teams, and keeping clear records.

  • Risk assessment of wards, kitchens, stores and waste areas
  • Low-risk methods in patient and treatment areas
  • Cockroach, ant, fly and rodent control
  • Monitoring points and trend reports
  • Coordination with infection control and facilities teams
  • Records of products, areas and times

Principles

How we plan healthcare pest control

Prevention first

Sealing gaps, managing waste and fixing moisture reduce the need to treat at all.

Low-risk methods

Baits in cracks and hiding places, traps and monitoring are preferred in sensitive areas.

Agreed access

We agree timing, access and protective equipment with your teams, and respect restricted areas.

Documented work

Every visit records the areas, the products and the times, so your team has a full record.

Working rules

What we agree before the first visit

A short written agreement keeps everyone clear on how pest control works on your site:

  • Who our technicians report to on arrival
  • Areas that are restricted or need an escort
  • Times when each area can be visited
  • Personal protective equipment and hygiene rules for technicians
  • How products are stored and carried on site
  • How staff report a sighting and how quickly we attend

Why it matters

Pests are a hygiene issue in healthcare

Cockroaches, flies and rodents can carry contamination from drains and waste onto surfaces and food. In a healthcare setting, where patients may be vulnerable, that is a hygiene risk as well as a nuisance.

Good control reduces the need for chemical treatments in clinical areas by keeping pests out in the first place: sealed entry points, controlled waste, dry floors and clean kitchens.

Records

What the pest control file should contain

Your quality and infection control teams may ask for these:

  • The contract and scope of service
  • A site plan with numbered monitoring points
  • A service report for each visit, with areas and times
  • A list of products used, with labels and safety data sheets
  • The provider's qualification and technician details
  • A log of sightings, response and corrective actions

Check your provider

A pest control provider should be ready to show its credentials. Ask any provider to show you its Balady qualification, and check that public health pesticides carry an SFDA registration number on the label.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do you spray in patient rooms?

In patient and treatment areas we prefer monitoring and low-risk methods such as baits in cracks and traps. Any treatment is agreed in advance with your team, and some areas may be excluded entirely.

Can you work around infection control rules?

Yes. We agree access, timing, personal protective equipment and any restricted areas with your infection control and facilities teams before work starts.

How do you report what products were used?

The service report lists the areas treated, the products used with their registration numbers, and the times, so your team has a full record.

Which pests are a concern in healthcare?

Cockroaches, ants, flies and rodents are the usual ones. Cafeterias, kitchens, waste areas and stores are common sources.

Do you serve small clinics as well as hospitals?

Yes. A small clinic needs a simpler plan, usually focused on waiting areas, staff kitchens, stores and waste, and we scale the programme to suit.
