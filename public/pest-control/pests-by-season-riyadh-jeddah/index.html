About this guide

What this guide covers

Pests do not take a season off in Saudi Arabia, but their behaviour changes with temperature, humidity and rain. This guide explains the general pattern in Riyadh and Jeddah. Individual buildings vary a lot, so use it as a planning aid rather than a forecast.

Two climates

Hot and dry Riyadh, humid coastal Jeddah

Riyadh is inland, in the desert. Summers are very hot and dry, winters are cooler, especially at night, and rain is occasional. Jeddah is on the Red Sea coast, so it is warm all year and noticeably more humid, with mild winters.

For pests, the difference comes down to water and shelter. In Riyadh, pests are drawn to anything that offers water and shade, such as drains, irrigation, air-conditioner condensate and kitchens. In Jeddah, humidity makes moisture-loving pests and mosquitoes more of a year-round concern. In both cities, air-conditioned buildings give pests a comfortable place to live all year.

All year

Pests that do not follow the calendar

Cockroaches

Active indoors all year, near kitchens, drains and warm corners. Heat and humidity can increase their numbers, but the source of food and water is the real driver.

Rodents

Look for shelter, food and water in every season. Colder spells can push them into buildings, and stores and roof spaces suit them year-round.

Bed bugs

Not seasonal. They travel in luggage and furniture, so travel and holiday periods can bring cases.

Termites

Work continuously under and inside buildings, wherever there is soil contact and moisture. Irrigation and leaking pipes raise the risk.

The common factor

Water matters more than the calendar

Air-conditioner condensate

Dripping lines and puddles give insects a reliable water source in dry weather.

Irrigation and leaks

Damp soil near walls attracts termites, ants and cockroaches.

Standing water

Pot saucers, buckets, tyres and uncovered tanks let mosquitoes breed within days.

Drains

Warm, wet and full of food residue, drains are cockroach habitat in both cities.

A year-round plan

A simple yearly checklist

A few checks at the right time prevent most problems:

  • Early spring: inspect for termites, nests in eaves and roof spaces, and gaps around doors
  • Before summer: service drains, seal gaps around pipes and check bins and the waste area
  • Summer: remove standing water weekly and keep kitchens and stores clean
  • After rain: look for puddles, blocked gutters and winged termites near lights
  • Early winter: proof doors and vents against rodents seeking warmth
  • Once a year: a professional inspection of the whole property

The building matters more than the city

Two buildings in the same street can have very different pest problems because of drains, waste, irrigation and how well they are sealed. If you are unsure, a site inspection is the quickest way to know what applies to your property.

When to call

Signs that mean you should call us

Do not wait for the next season if you notice:

  • Cockroaches seen in daylight or in several rooms
  • Droppings, gnawing or noises in ceilings
  • Mud tubes, hollow-sounding wood or discarded wings
  • Bites in lines or clusters, or blood spots on bedding
  • Mosquitoes in numbers indoors or around the garden
  • Birds nesting on the roof, or flies breeding around waste

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is there a pest-free season?

No. Indoor pests such as cockroaches, rodents and bed bugs can be active all year, because buildings stay warm and food and water are always available. Seasons change what is most active and where.

Are mosquitoes a problem in Riyadh as well as Jeddah?

Mosquitoes need standing water to breed, so any property with pools of water, leaking pipes or irrigation can have them. Humid coastal areas tend to have more favourable conditions, but the breeding sites decide the risk.

When should I check for termites?

Termites work all year inside and under buildings. Winged termites near lights are often seen at certain times of year, but the safe approach is an inspection once a year, or whenever you see mud tubes or hollow-sounding wood.

When is the best time to book a treatment?

Before the problem peaks. A check in spring ahead of the hot months is a sensible habit, and a check after heavy rain helps find new standing water.

Does the weather affect how long treatment lasts?

Heat, sun, rain and cleaning can all affect how long a treatment stays effective outdoors. We explain what to expect for your site when we quote.
