Early warning

Signs of termites in a building

Termites stay hidden, so the signs are easy to miss. Call for an inspection if you see any of these:

  • Mud tubes, the width of a pencil, on walls, skirting or foundations
  • Wood that sounds hollow when tapped
  • Blistered paint or a rippled surface on door frames and cabinets
  • Small piles of discarded wings near windows or lights
  • Doors or windows that begin to stick
  • Fine soil or mud packed into joints and cracks

How it happens

How termites get into buildings

The termites that damage buildings here are mainly subterranean. They nest in the soil and travel to timber through cracks in the floor slab, expansion joints, and gaps around pipes and cable conduits.

They build mud tubes to stay protected from dry air and light, which is why the damage is often well advanced before anyone sees an insect. Door frames, skirting, wardrobes and kitchen cabinets are usually attacked first.

Because the colony is in the ground, spraying the visible insects does not solve the problem. Treatment has to put a treated zone between the soil and the building, or eliminate the colony through baiting.

For developers and contractors

Pre-construction anti-termite treatment

Timing

Soil treatment is applied after final compaction and before the vapour barrier and slab. Tell us your pour dates early so it does not hold up the programme.

Perimeter

The external perimeter is treated after backfilling and final grading, so the treated zone is not disturbed by later earthworks.

Pricing

What affects the price of termite treatment

We measure the building at the inspection and quote in writing. The price depends on:

  • The treated area or the length of walls to be treated
  • Construction type and floor finishes
  • How far the activity has spread
  • The method: barrier, baiting or both
  • The warranty period you ask for

Ask for it in writing

Before you agree to any termite treatment, ask for the product name and its registration number, the areas to be treated, the quantity to be applied, and the warranty terms. A reputable provider gives you all four.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I have termites?

Common signs are mud tubes, hollow-sounding wood, discarded wings and damaged skirting or door frames. An inspection confirms it.

Can termites be prevented in a new building?

Pre-construction treatment of the soil is commonly done before the slab is poured. Contact us early in the project.

How long does treatment take?

It depends on the size of the property and the infestation. We confirm the scope and timing after the inspection.

Is drilling through my floor tiles noticeable?

Holes are small, placed along joints where possible, and sealed with matching material after injection.

Do I need to move out during treatment?

Usually not. Rooms stay in use and we tell you if any area needs to be vacated for a short time.

How long does protection last?

It depends on the product, the soil and the method. The warranty period is stated in the quotation, so ask to see it in writing.

Is baiting better than a chemical barrier?

They do different jobs. A barrier protects the building quickly; baiting targets the colony and suits sites where drilling is not possible. We sometimes recommend both.
