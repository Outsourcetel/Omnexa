Planning

A simple plan through the year

Before summer

Check for termite activity, seal entry points and treat the garden and drains before the hottest weeks.

During summer

Keep an eye on standing water, kitchen hygiene and flies. Call early if you see cockroaches.

Around wet weather

After rain, check for standing water, blocked drains and winged termites near lights.

Annual check

A yearly inspection catches termite and rodent problems before they reach the structure.

Before we arrive

How to prepare your villa

A little preparation makes treatment more effective and safer for your household:

  • Tell us about children, pets, fish tanks, pregnancy or respiratory conditions
  • Clear under sinks and from the kitchen cupboards to be treated
  • Cover or put away food, utensils, baby items and toys
  • Move pet food, bowls and cages out of the area
  • Keep children and pets out of treated areas for the period we advise
  • Ventilate as instructed, and keep the service report

Termites

Termite risk in villas

Villas have soil contact all around the building, and gardens, irrigation and leaking pipes keep the ground damp near walls. Subterranean termites can enter through the slab, expansion joints and gaps around pipes, then attack door frames, skirting and cabinets.

The signs are easy to miss: mud tubes, hollow-sounding wood, blistered paint and discarded wings. An inspection every year is the practical protection, and treatment before or after construction creates a barrier. See our termite control page for the options.

Compounds

How compound programmes work

A compound is easier to protect when treatment is coordinated:

  • A survey of shared areas, drains, waste points and landscaping
  • A schedule agreed with management and communicated to residents
  • Treatment of shared areas on the same visit as villa treatments
  • A central record of visits and any resident reports
  • A route for residents to report a problem
  • Regular review of recurring locations

Mosquitoes around the home

Mosquitoes breed in small amounts of standing water: pot saucers, buckets, blocked gutters and uncovered tanks. Removing standing water is the first step, and fogging works best alongside it. See our mosquito control page for the full approach.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe for children and pets?

Tell us about children, pets, fish tanks and any health conditions when you book. We choose products and methods to suit and tell you how long to keep people and pets out of treated areas.

Do we need to move out during treatment?

Usually not. We tell you in advance if an area needs to be empty for a short time, and give a clear re-entry time.

How often should a villa be treated?

It depends on the pests, the garden and the surroundings. Some households treat seasonally and others only when a problem appears. We advise after the inspection.

Can compound management arrange treatment for all villas?

Yes. We can coordinate a schedule with management, so shared areas and individual villas are treated together and residents are informed in advance.

Should I worry about termites in a villa?

Termites are a risk to door frames, cabinets and timber in any building with soil contact. An inspection tells you if there is activity, and a plan tells you how to protect the property.
