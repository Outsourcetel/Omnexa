Mosquito / Dengue Control: what we do

Some mosquitoes carry disease, and the Aedes mosquito that spreads dengue breeds in small amounts of standing water around homes and buildings. Fogging alone does not solve it. Effective control finds and removes the breeding sites, treats water that cannot be removed, and then targets adult mosquitoes.

  • Breeding-site inspection of gardens, roofs, tanks and drains
  • Larvicide treatment of water that cannot be drained
  • Adult mosquito treatment by fogging and barrier spraying
  • Advice on removing breeding sites
  • Programmes for compounds, hotels, schools and construction sites
  • Follow-up inspections and monitoring

Know your pest

How mosquitoes develop, and where to break the cycle

Mosquitoes spend the first part of their life in water. That is where control works best.

StageWhere it livesWhat it means for control
EggOn the wet edge of containers, just above the waterlineEggs can survive dry spells and hatch when water returns. Scrub containers, do not just tip them out
Larva and pupaIn standing water, for roughly a week or two in warm weatherThe easiest stage to control: empty the water, cover it, or treat it with larvicide
AdultResting in shade, vegetation and dark corners; biting peopleTreated with fogging or barrier spraying, which gives short-term relief

Aedes mosquitoes, which can spread dengue, typically bite during the day and breed in small containers close to people.

Inspection

Where we look for breeding sites

Mosquitoes need very little water. We check:

  • Plant pot saucers, buckets, tyres and discarded containers
  • Roof gutters and flat roofs where water collects
  • Water tanks, cisterns and covers
  • Floor drains, manholes and sumps
  • Air-conditioner drip trays and condensate drains
  • Fountains, ponds and ornamental water
  • Construction sites, excavations and open pipes

What you can do

Weekly habits that reduce mosquitoes

Most of the control work is routine. These take a few minutes a week.

  • Empty and scrub containers that hold water
  • Cover water tanks and check the covers are intact
  • Keep gutters and drains clear
  • Check air-conditioner drip trays
  • Change water in pet bowls and vases frequently
  • Keep window and door screens in good repair

For compounds, hotels, schools and construction sites

Large sites need a programme, not a one-off visit. We map the site, set a schedule for inspection and larvicide treatment, and report each visit so the property manager can see what was done.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do mosquitoes breed around a home?

Females lay eggs in standing water. Pot saucers, buckets, tyres, blocked gutters, uncovered tanks and drains are common places. Some species can breed in a bottle cap of water.

Is fogging enough?

No. Fogging kills adult mosquitoes that are flying at the time, but new ones emerge from the breeding sites within days. It works best as one part of a plan that removes and treats the breeding sites.

Is the treatment safe around people and pets?

Tell us about children, pets, fish ponds and plants when you book. We choose products and methods to suit, and tell you how long to stay out of treated areas.

How often should treatment be repeated?

It depends on the season and the site. In warm weather, programmes usually check breeding sites regularly, because new water collects after rain or watering.

Can you treat a construction site?

Yes. Excavations, open pipes and stored water are common breeding places. We set a schedule and report each visit to the site manager.
