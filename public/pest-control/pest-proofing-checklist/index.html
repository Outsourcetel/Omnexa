About this guide

What this guide covers

Pests need three things: food, water and shelter. Proofing your property takes those away and closes the entry points. Most of it is simple maintenance you can do yourself, and it makes professional treatment far more effective.

  • Entry points to seal
  • Food, waste and storage habits
  • Water and drainage checks
  • Outdoor and garden measures
  • A weekly, monthly and yearly routine

Entry points

Close the gaps pests use

A mouse can use a gap about the width of a pencil. Check each of these.

WhereWhat to checkFix
External doorsGaps at the bottom and sides, doors left openFit door sweeps and seals, keep doors closed
Windows and ventsMissing or torn screens, uncovered ventsRepair screens and fit mesh over vents
Pipes and cablesGaps where they pass through walls and floorsSeal with suitable filler or metal mesh
Roof and eavesGaps under tiles, open eaves, nesting sitesClose gaps and fit bird deterrents where needed
Floor and wall cracksCracks in the slab, skirting and expansion jointsSeal and, if signs of termites appear, call for an inspection
Drains and manholesMissing or loose coversFit secure covers or traps
Air-conditioner platforms and ductsOpen penetrations and unscreened openingsSeal around pipework and screen openings

Kitchen and food

Take away the food source

Keeping food out of reach does more than any spray:

  • Store dry food in sealed containers
  • Clean spills and crumbs the same day, including behind appliances
  • Do not leave pet food out overnight
  • Empty the kitchen bin daily and use a lidded bin
  • Wash dishes before bed and do not leave dirty plates out overnight
  • Keep fruit in the fridge or a closed container

Water

Remove standing and leaking water

Fix leaks

Dripping taps, pipes and air-conditioner condensate lines give pests a constant supply of water.

Empty standing water

Pot saucers, buckets, tyres and uncovered tanks are mosquito breeding sites.

Keep drains clear

Clean floor drains and fit covers. Run water through seldom-used drains.

Check irrigation

Wet soil beside walls attracts termites, so direct irrigation away from the building.

Waste and storage

Manage waste and storage

Waste and clutter give pests shelter and food:

  • Use bins with tight lids and keep them away from doors
  • Take waste out daily and clean the bin area
  • Keep stores tidy, with items off the floor and away from walls
  • Break down cardboard quickly and remove it
  • Clear unused items from roof spaces, garages and maid's rooms
  • Do not store firewood, timber or boxes against exterior walls

Outdoors

Garden and exterior measures

What happens outside decides what comes in:

  • Trim plants and branches that touch the building
  • Keep soil and mulch below the damp-proof course
  • Remove fallen fruit and garden waste
  • Keep gutters and drains clear
  • Store timber and building materials off the ground
  • Cover water tanks and fit tight covers on manholes

A routine

What to do, and how often

A short routine keeps the property protected.

How oftenWhat to do
DailyEmpty kitchen bins, clean spills, wipe surfaces, close external doors
WeeklyEmpty standing water, take waste out, clean drains and check the garden
MonthlyCheck door seals, screens, vents and under sinks for leaks and droppings
SeasonalCheck the roof, eaves and entry points, and review the plan before the hot months
YearlyBook a professional inspection of the whole property, including for termites

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I pest-proof my property myself?

Many measures, such as storing food properly, managing waste, fixing leaks and fitting door sweeps, are simple to do yourself. Larger gaps, roof proofing and treatments for established infestations are better done with professional help.

What is the most important thing to do?

Remove food, water and shelter. Sealed food storage, dry floors and sinks, covered bins and a clear, tidy store make the biggest difference.

Does proofing replace pest control?

No. Proofing reduces the risk and helps treatment work, but an existing infestation still needs treating. The two work together.

Who is responsible for proofing in a rented property?

It depends on your tenancy agreement and on whether the problem is in the structure or in how the space is used. Report structural gaps to the landlord or building management in writing.

Is there anything to do when building or renovating?

Yes. Sealing around pipes and cables, fitting door sweeps and vent covers, and planning termite protection before the slab is poured are far easier at this stage.
