What we cover

Pest control for food service

A pest sighting in a restaurant damages trust faster than almost anything else. Kitchens offer food, warmth, water and hiding places, so control has to be continuous, planned around service hours and kept away from food.

  • Inspection of kitchens, stores, bins and drains
  • Cockroach, fly, ant and rodent control
  • Out-of-hours visits that avoid service times
  • Numbered monitoring points on a site plan
  • Fly control units for kitchens and dining areas
  • Service reports after every visit

The programme

How a restaurant programme works

Survey and site plan

We inspect, grade the risk areas and number every monitoring point on a plan.

Out-of-hours visits

Visits are arranged before opening or after closing, so service is not interrupted.

Methods suited to food areas

Baits go into cracks and hiding places away from food, and monitoring replaces spraying wherever possible.

Reports and review

A report follows every visit, and the records are reviewed so the programme is adjusted.

Your team

Daily habits that keep pests out

Pest control works best alongside good housekeeping. These habits make the biggest difference:

  • Clean up spills and food residue at the end of every shift, including under and behind equipment
  • Empty bins daily, keep them lidded and clean the bin area
  • Keep stored food off the floor, in sealed containers
  • Break down cardboard and remove it quickly, as it shelters cockroaches
  • Keep back and delivery doors closed when not in use
  • Report any sighting immediately and write it in the sighting log

Inspection ready

The records an inspector or auditor expects

Whether the visitor is a municipal inspector or a food-safety auditor, the questions are similar. Keep these in one file:

  • The service contract and scope
  • A site plan showing every monitoring point
  • A service report for each visit
  • A list of products used, with their labels
  • The provider's qualification and technician details
  • A sighting log completed by your staff
  • A record of corrective actions and when they were closed

By format

Restaurants, cafes and cloud kitchens differ

Full-service restaurants

Larger kitchens, more storage and staff turnover. Regular visits and drain and waste area attention matter most.

Cafes and bakeries

Sweet and dry stock draws ants, flies and stored-product insects. Sealed storage and entry-point sealing come first.

Central and cloud kitchens

High volume, long working hours and bulk deliveries. Monitoring points, receiving-area control and trend reports matter most.

Food courts and shared kitchens

Several tenants share drains and waste areas, so coordination between tenants decides the result.

Check your provider

A pest control provider should be ready to show its credentials. Ask any provider to show you its Balady qualification, and check that public health pesticides carry an SFDA registration number on the label.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you treat while we are open?

Most work can be scheduled before opening or after closing. We plan visits around your service hours and tell you if any area needs to stay closed for a short time.

Is the treatment safe around food?

We choose methods suited to food areas, such as gel baits placed in cracks and hiding places and non-toxic monitoring, and we keep products away from food and food contact surfaces. Tell us which areas handle open food.

How often will you visit a restaurant?

It depends on the size of the kitchen, the risk and the findings. We propose a frequency after the survey and adjust it using the monitoring records.

What do we do if a customer sees a cockroach?

Call us straight away. We attend, find where it came from and treat the source. Please record the date, time and location in your sighting log.

Do you work with cafes and cloud kitchens too?

Yes. A small cafe, a cloud kitchen and a central kitchen need different visit patterns, and we plan each one separately.
