Building Maintenance: what we do

Buildings work best when problems are found early. A maintenance programme replaces emergency repairs with a schedule of checks, a clear way to report faults and records you can use. The scope is agreed with you for each site, covering the services we provide.

  • Planned preventive maintenance schedule
  • Reactive call-outs between planned visits
  • Checklists for each area and system
  • Written reports after visits
  • AC and HVAC maintenance within the programme
  • Coordination with pest control, safety signage and works

The approach

Planned maintenance first, call-outs for the rest

Most building problems start small: a blocked AC drain, a worn sign, a gap that lets pests in. Planned preventive maintenance is a schedule of checks at set intervals, so these are found while they are small and before they interrupt your operations.

Reactive call-outs cover what happens between visits. A good programme keeps both in one record, so repeated call-outs for the same fault show up and the cause can be fixed instead of the symptom.

Between visits

How a reactive call-out is handled

Log

You report the fault by phone or WhatsApp. We record the location, what happened and when.

Attend

A technician is sent with the right skills for the fault, and we tell you when to expect them.

Fix or report

Where the fault is within scope it is repaired. If it needs parts or a separate quotation, we explain before proceeding.

Close and record

The fault is closed in the log with what was found and done, so repeats can be spotted.

Checklists

What a building walk-round checks

Checklists keep every visit consistent. A typical walk-round covers:

  • AC units: running, noise, filters, drains and leaks
  • Safety signs: present, visible, undamaged and photoluminescent signs not obstructed
  • Safety equipment: stored correctly and in usable condition
  • Pest control: monitoring points intact and any new signs of activity
  • Visible condition of finishes, doors and fittings, noting defects for referral
  • Access routes and exits kept clear and marked

Reporting

What a maintenance report should contain

A report is useful only if it says exactly what was done and what you should do next.

  • Date, site, areas visited and the technician's name
  • The checklist with each item marked as satisfactory or needing attention
  • Faults found, with photos and locations
  • Work completed during the visit
  • Recommendations and items needing a separate quotation
  • Open items carried over from previous visits
  • The date of the next planned visit

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between preventive and reactive maintenance?

Preventive maintenance is planned: scheduled checks and servicing to stop faults developing. Reactive maintenance is the response when something fails or a fault is reported. A good programme has both.

Which systems can be included?

The services we provide: AC and HVAC, construction and fit-out works, safety signage and equipment, and pest control. We agree the exact scope with you for each site.

What if you find a problem outside the agreed scope?

We record it, take photos and tell you, so you can decide who should deal with it. Nothing outside the scope is started without your agreement.

How do I report a fault between visits?

By phone or WhatsApp. We log it, confirm the details and arrange attendance. Photos and a short description help us send the right person.

Can one programme cover several buildings?

Yes. Multi-site programmes use the same checklists and report format at each site so you can compare them.
