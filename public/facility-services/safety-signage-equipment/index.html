Safety Signage and Equipment: what we do

Good signs tell people what to do and where to go when it matters, and the right protective equipment protects the people doing the work. We supply both, and help you choose what suits your site.

  • Warning signs
  • Prohibition signs
  • Mandatory signs
  • Safe condition signs
  • Photoluminescent (glow-in-the-dark) signs
  • Safety helmets, gloves, eye protection, high-visibility clothing, safety footwear and harnesses

Sign types

The four main types of safety sign

Colour and shape tell people what a sign means before they read it.

TypeLookMeaningExamples
WarningYellow triangle with a black symbolA hazard is present: take careSlippery floor, overhead load, electrical hazard, forklift traffic
ProhibitionRed circle with a diagonal barThis action is not allowedNo entry, no smoking, no mobile phones, do not touch
MandatoryBlue circle with a white symbolThis action must be takenWear a helmet, wear gloves, wear eye protection, wash hands
Safe conditionGreen rectangle or squareShows an exit, route or safe pointExit, assembly point, first aid, escape route direction

Use the same colours and symbols throughout a site so that staff and visitors learn them once.

In the dark

Photoluminescent signs

Photoluminescent signs contain a material that absorbs light from lamps or daylight and then releases it slowly. When the lights go out, the sign stays visible in the dark for a period without any power supply.

They are used on escape routes, stairwells, exit doors and other places where people must find their way if the lighting fails. They need to be placed where they receive enough light to recharge, and they should not be covered or blocked. How long they glow depends on the material and the light they received, so ask us for the details of the product you are considering.

Placement

Where signs should go

A sign only works if it is seen at the right moment. As a guide:

  • Place warning signs before the hazard, at a distance that gives time to react
  • Put mandatory signs at the point where the equipment must be worn
  • Place prohibition signs at entrances and on the object or area concerned
  • Mount exit and route signs where they can be seen along the whole escape route
  • Keep signs at a consistent height and in clear view, not behind doors or stock
  • Repeat key signs in the languages your workforce reads

Choosing

How to choose the right equipment

Equipment that does not fit or suit the task will not be worn. Work through these steps:

  • List the tasks and the hazards in each, starting from a risk assessment
  • Match each hazard to the type of protection that addresses it
  • Check the product is marked as meeting a recognised standard for that protection
  • Choose sizes that fit and styles that are comfortable in hot conditions
  • Make sure items work together, such as a helmet with eye protection
  • Train staff to use, inspect and store equipment, and replace anything damaged
